Episode 02: Time Travel Paranoia#

The girl who should be dead walks into the lecture hall. Time Travel Paranoia introduces the series' central question: if the world changed, why is Okabe the only one who noticed?

Synopsis#

Kurisu Makise corners Okabe after a lecture, demanding to know what he meant to tell her at Radio Kaikan — a conversation Okabe never had, in this worldline. His frantic reaction ("you should be dead") does not improve her opinion of him.

Back at the lab, Daru demonstrates the Phone Microwave's one reliable behavior: bananas placed inside it turn into an unnatural green gel, and — stranger — the gel bananas reattach themselves to the bunch left across the room. Okabe starts a notebook. Column one: things that should be impossible.

Meanwhile the @channel boards light up. A poster claiming to be John Titor — a name Okabe remembers from the year 2000 — has begun posting for the first time, in 2010, describing a dystopian 2036 ruled by SERN and asking for help locating an IBN 5100.

Nobody else remembers Titor's 2000 posts. In this worldline they never happened. Okabe's memory and the world's record now disagree — the first hard evidence of what the Divergence Meter will later quantify.

🕰️John Titor's claims

SERN achieved time travel and used it to seize power. A surveillance dystopia followed. Titor claims to be a resistance courier sent back to recover an IBN 5100, the only machine able to decode SERN's oldest archives.

Titor's model matches the many-worlds interpretation loosely, but with a twist the series makes canonical: worldlines converge. Small changes are absorbed; only changes past a threshold jump between attractor fields. He calls the constant measuring this the divergence number — see divergence-meter.

Titor insists time machines cannot cross between attractor fields. Events of the finale prove this incomplete — the barrier can be crossed, at a price. Treat his posts as in-universe primary sources, not physics.

🔬Analysis

The gel banana is the episode's quiet masterstroke. It establishes — before any character says "time travel" aloud — that the Phone Microwave transports matter destructively. The audience is being told, one season early, why a human must never be sent.1 When SERN's own experiments surface in episode-03, the gel state already has a name in the viewer's head.

The divergence reading holds steady at $d=0.571015$ this episode: paranoia, not physics, is doing the work.

🔗Continuity
  • The IBN 5100 is named for the first time. It resurfaces in episode-04, episode-05, and episode-08.
  • Kurisu's lecture topic — memory as neural data — foreshadows the Time Leap Machine she builds in episode-11.
  • Okabe's notebook becomes the seed of the lab's D-Mail log; see the D-Mail record.
  • First mention of Mr. Braun, the lab's landlord.

The @channel poster is Suzuha Amane, posting under her mission alias. Her father is a lab member. The series does not confirm this until Dogma in Event Horizon.
