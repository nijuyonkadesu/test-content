Episode 04: Interpreter Rendezvous#

Lab Member 004 gets her badge, a journalist who never speaks above a text message appears, and the hunt for the IBN 5100 begins in earnest.

Synopsis#

Kurisu accepts a lab member badge — number 004 — with the enthusiasm of a hostage, and immediately proves indispensable by redesigning the Phone Microwave's send procedure so a D-Mail no longer requires someone to physically kick the microwave door.

Okabe, following Titor's posts, resolves to find the IBN 5100 before SERN's collaborators do. The trail leads through Akihabara's retro-computing shops to a name Okabe knows: the Yanabayashi Shrine, where a "strange old computer" was donated decades ago.

At a shop counter he meets Moeka Kiryu, a freelance editor who communicates almost exclusively by mail even when standing in front of you — and who is, by coincidence she does not explain, also looking for an IBN 5100.1

Moeka's mail-only affect reads as comedy on first viewing. On rewatch it is the episode's loudest alarm. See the spoiler fold below.

🖥️The IBN 5100

The IBN 5100 ships with an undocumented mode that can read SERN's earliest mainframe formats. SERN needs one to decode its own past; the lab needs one to decode SERN. Two search parties, one machine, one shrine.

Donated to the Yanabayashi Shrine years ago and forgotten in its storeroom. Ruka Urushibara, the shrine family's child, appears in the next episode to hand it over.

Spoiler-adjacent: the machine's availability is worldline-dependent. The D-Mail sent in episode-08 retroactively removes it from the shrine — the single most consequential change of the season. Tracked in detail at Alpha worldline: divergence readings.

🔬Analysis

The episode's title is literal: everything is interpretation. Moeka interprets the lab as a lead; Okabe interprets her as a fellow enthusiast; Kurisu interprets the badge as a joke and pockets it anyway. The script lets every misreading stand, because on the Alpha worldline ($d=0.571015$) misreadings are load-bearing: the truth would end the story here.

A quiet formal touch: this is the first episode with no on-screen D-Mail and no divergence movement — the calm before the experiments begin.

🔗Continuity
  • First appearance of Moeka Kiryu and first mention of Faris's cafe, May Queen Nyan-Nyan.
  • The badge ceremony mirrors, shot for shot, the one in episode-22.
  • Okabe calls the microwave "FG-08" on screen for the first time — FG standing for Future Gadget.

Moeka is a Rounder — a SERN field agent — and her handler is reachable at the address she calls "FB". Her search for the IBN 5100 is a SERN retrieval op. The friendship she strikes with the lab is real and an assignment; the series makes both true at once, which is why episode-12 lands so hard.
