Akihabara — Akiba to everyone who has been twice — is Tokyo's electric town: a district of component shops, anime storefronts, maid cafes, and arcades stacked vertically until the signage becomes the architecture. It is the home ground of the Future Gadget Laboratory and the stage for nearly every event this wiki documents. When worldlines shift, ==Akihabara is where the difference shows first== — and in one infamous case, the district itself was the difference.

🏮Character

Akiba runs on two economies. Above ground: flagship stores, character goods, billboards that replace themselves seasonally, the crowd flowing from the station down Chuo-dori on foot when the street closes to cars. Below the signage: the older metabolism of component stalls and junk shops — bins of salvaged capacitors, one-man counters that can source a vacuum tube discontinued before the customer was born. The lab is a creature of the second economy; the Divergence Meter's Nixie tubes, the Helicam's camera, and most of the FG line's organs were bought within a ten-minute radius of its door.

The district's texture is why the lab could exist unnoticed for so long: in Akihabara, a person carrying a raygun-shaped remote control and twenty meters of coolant hose is below the threshold of remark.

🌀Worldline sensitivity

Akihabara's present character is recent — a layer of otaku commerce deposited over an older radio-parts district in a single generation. This made it terrifyingly responsive to D-Mail interference. The lab's documented worldline shifts altered, at various divergence values: which stores anchored which corners, whether a landmark building bore a satellite through its roof, and — at the deepest recorded excursion — ==whether the otaku layer had happened at all==. On the 0.33-band worldline, lab members walked out into an Akihabara of gray electronics wholesalers, the moe signage gone as if scrubbed, and understood viscerally what the attractor field pages mean by "detail." 📍Sites of interest

Radio Kaikan — the district's vertical heart and the site of the story's inciting impossibility. Akihabara Station — everyone's entrance and several partings. The riverside walk toward Yanabayashi Shrine, where the noise falls away block by block.

The Braun Tube Workshop with the lab above it; May Queen Nyan-Nyan, the maid cafe where half the lab's meetings actually happen; the vending machine corner that has sold Dk Pepper on every recorded worldline, a constancy lab members find genuinely comforting. #lore

Storefronts that exist only in one man's memory: a crepe stand, a rental showcase shop, an arcade with exactly one good cabinet. Okabe's list is in the lab notebook under "places I owe money in worldlines that no longer exist."

Akiba's crowd is the lab's camouflage and its dread. Every SERN tail was lost in it; one was spotted because he stood still in it, which nobody in Akihabara does. The handbook's tradecraft section is one line: "Move like you're late for a preorder."
