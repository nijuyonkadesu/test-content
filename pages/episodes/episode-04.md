# Episode 04: Interpreter Rendezvous

Lab Member 004 gets her badge, a journalist who never speaks above a text
message appears, and the hunt for the IBN 5100 begins in earnest.

:::::columns{ratio="65-35"}
::::column

## Synopsis

[[kurisu|Kurisu]] accepts a [[lab-member-badge|lab member badge]] — number
004 — with the enthusiasm of a hostage, and immediately proves indispensable
by redesigning the [[phone-microwave|Phone Microwave]]'s send procedure so a
D-Mail no longer requires someone to physically kick the microwave door.

Okabe, following [[wp:John Titor|Titor]]'s posts, resolves to find the
IBN 5100 before SERN's collaborators do. The trail leads through
[[akihabara|Akihabara]]'s retro-computing shops to a name Okabe knows:
the [[yanabayashi-shrine|Yanabayashi Shrine]], where a "strange old
computer" was donated decades ago.

At a shop counter he meets [[moeka-kiryu|Moeka Kiryu]], a freelance editor
who communicates almost exclusively by mail even when standing in front of
you — and who is, by coincidence she does not explain, *also* looking for an
IBN 5100.[^1]

:::note
Moeka's mail-only affect reads as comedy on first viewing. On rewatch it is
the episode's loudest alarm. See the spoiler fold below.
:::

::section[The IBN 5100]{icon="🖥️"}

:::::tabs
:::tab{label="Why it matters"}
The IBN 5100 ships with an undocumented mode that can read SERN's earliest
mainframe formats. SERN needs one to decode its own past; the lab needs one
to decode SERN. Two search parties, one machine, one shrine.
:::
:::tab{label="Where it is"}
Donated to the [[yanabayashi-shrine|Yanabayashi Shrine]] years ago and
forgotten in its storeroom. [[ruka-urushibara|Ruka Urushibara]], the shrine
family's child, appears in [[episode-05|the next episode]] to hand it over.
:::
::::tab{label="Where it goes"}
:::warning
Spoiler-adjacent: the machine's *availability* is worldline-dependent. The
D-Mail sent in [[episode-08]] retroactively removes it from the shrine — the
single most consequential change of the season. Tracked in detail at
[[alpha-worldline#Divergence readings|Alpha worldline: divergence readings]].
:::

::section[Analysis]{icon="🔬"}

The episode's title is literal: everything is interpretation. Moeka
interprets the lab as a lead; Okabe interprets her as a fellow enthusiast;
Kurisu interprets the badge as a joke and pockets it anyway. The script lets
every misreading stand, because on the
[[alpha-worldline|Alpha worldline]] ($d=0.571015$) misreadings are load-bearing:
the truth would end the story here.

A quiet formal touch: this is the first episode with no on-screen D-Mail and
no divergence movement — the calm before ==the experiments begin==.

::section[Continuity]{icon="🔗"}

- First appearance of [[moeka-kiryu|Moeka Kiryu]] and first mention of
  [[faris-nyannyan|Faris]]'s cafe, [[may-queen-nyan-nyan|May Queen Nyan-Nyan]].
- The badge ceremony mirrors, shot for shot, the one in [[episode-22]].
- Okabe calls the microwave "FG-08" on screen for the first time —
  FG standing for Future Gadget.

:::collapse{title="Spoilers — who Moeka answers to"}
Moeka is a Rounder — a SERN field agent — and her handler is reachable at
the address she calls "FB". Her search for the IBN 5100 is a SERN retrieval
op. The friendship she strikes with the lab is real *and* an assignment; the
series makes both true at once, which is why [[episode-12]] lands so hard.
:::
