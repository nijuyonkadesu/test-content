# Episode 02: Time Travel Paranoia

The girl who should be dead walks into the lecture hall. *Time Travel
Paranoia* introduces the series' central question: if the world changed,
why is [[okabe|Okabe]] the only one who noticed?

:::::columns{ratio="65-35"}
::::column

## Synopsis

[[kurisu|Kurisu Makise]] corners Okabe after a lecture, demanding to know what
he meant to tell her at Radio Kaikan — a conversation Okabe never had, in
*this* worldline. His frantic reaction ("you should be dead") does not
improve her opinion of him.

Back at the [[future-gadget-lab|lab]], [[daru|Daru]] demonstrates the
[[phone-microwave|Phone Microwave]]'s one reliable behavior: bananas placed
inside it turn into an unnatural ==green gel==, and — stranger — the gel
bananas reattach themselves to the bunch left across the room. Okabe starts a
notebook. Column one: *things that should be impossible*.

Meanwhile the @channel boards light up. A poster claiming to be
[[wp:John Titor|John Titor]] — a name Okabe remembers from the year 2000 —
has begun posting *for the first time*, in 2010, describing a dystopian 2036
ruled by SERN and asking for help locating an IBN 5100.

:::warning
Nobody else remembers Titor's 2000 posts. In this worldline they never
happened. Okabe's memory and the world's record now disagree — the first
hard evidence of what the [[divergence-meter|Divergence Meter]] will later
quantify.
:::

::section[John Titor's claims]{icon="🕰️"}

:::::tabs
:::tab{label="The 2036 he describes"}
SERN achieved time travel and used it to seize power. A surveillance
dystopia followed. Titor claims to be a resistance courier sent back to
recover an IBN 5100, the only machine able to decode SERN's oldest archives.
:::
:::tab{label="The physics he cites"}
Titor's model matches the [[wp:Many-worlds interpretation|many-worlds
interpretation]] loosely, but with a twist the series makes canonical:
worldlines *converge*. Small changes are absorbed; only changes past a
threshold jump between [[worldlines|attractor fields]]. He calls the constant
measuring this the **divergence number** — see [[divergence-meter]].
:::
::::tab{label="What he gets wrong"}
:::danger
Titor insists time machines cannot cross between attractor fields. Events of
[[episode-22|the finale]] prove this incomplete — the barrier can be crossed,
at a price. Treat his posts as in-universe primary sources, not physics.
:::

::section[Analysis]{icon="🔬"}

:::figure{align="right" width="260"}
![Gel banana diagram](https://picsum.photos/seed/sg-ep02-gel/260/160 "The gel state: matter after a partial temporal transfer.")
:::

The gel banana is the episode's quiet masterstroke. It establishes — before
any character says "time travel" aloud — that the Phone Microwave transports
matter destructively. The audience is being told, one season early, why a
*human* must never be sent.[^1] When SERN's own experiments surface in
[[episode-03]], the gel state already has a name in the viewer's head.

The divergence reading holds steady at $d=0.571015$ this episode: paranoia,
not physics, is doing the work.

::section[Continuity]{icon="🔗"}

- The IBN 5100 is named for the first time. It resurfaces in
  [[episode-04]], [[episode-05]], and [[episode-08]].
- Kurisu's lecture topic — memory as neural data — foreshadows the
  [[time-leap-machine|Time Leap Machine]] she builds in [[episode-11]].
- Okabe's notebook becomes the seed of the lab's D-Mail log; see
  [[episode-guide#D-Mail record|the D-Mail record]].
- First mention of [[mr-braun|Mr. Braun]], the lab's landlord.

:::collapse{title="Spoilers — Titor's identity"}
The @channel poster is [[suzuha-amane|Suzuha Amane]], posting under her
mission alias. Her father is a lab member. The series does not confirm this
until [[episode-11|Dogma in Event Horizon]].
:::
