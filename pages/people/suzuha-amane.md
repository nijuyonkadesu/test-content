Suzuha Amane is a time traveler from the year 2036, sent to the present by a
resistance cell fighting a global order enforced by [[sern|SERN]]. She spends
most of 2010 undercover as a part-time clerk at Braun Tsuushin, the
CRT-repair shop run by an old man she knows only as her employer — and who
is, unbeknownst to either of them for most of the story, her own father,
[[yuugo-tennouji|Yuugo Tennouji]].

She later joins the [[future-gadget-laboratory|Future Gadget Laboratory]] as
Lab Member No. 004, bringing a soldier's directness that clashes, then
gels, with [[okabe|Okabe]]'s theatrics. Her mission is simple to state and
brutal to execute: stop the timeline that produces the world she was born
into — a world under martial rule, its populace surveilled through
technology descended from Future Gadget Lab research that SERN captured
and weaponized.

::section[Mission]{icon="🎯"}

:::::columns{ratio="60-40"}
::::column
Suzuha's future is one where SERN, having perfected time travel decades
ahead of anyone else, used that head start to consolidate total control.
Her cell's plan hinges on preventing SERN from ever recovering the
[[phone-microwave|Phone Microwave]]'s underlying research — which means
staying close to the one lab reckless enough to keep reinventing it. She
carries a time machine capable of one-way jumps into the past; getting home
again is a separate, much harder problem she does not discuss with the lab
until she has to.

Her public cover story borrows the name **John Titor** — a nod to an old
internet legend about a soldier from 2036 who claimed to have traveled back
looking for an IBN 5100-class computer.[^titor] Lab members who catch the
reference never quite believe the coincidence is a coincidence.
::::
::::column
:::note
Suzuha never volunteers her real objective outright. Most of what the lab
learns about 2036 comes out sideways, in offhand remarks she immediately
tries to walk back.
:::

:::tip
Her bicycle isn't decoration — in at least one worldline it's how she wins
a race against a car through central [[akihabara|Akihabara]], and how she
buys Okabe the seconds he needs at the climax of the July incident.
:::

::section[Personality]{icon="🎭"}

Blunt, athletic, and allergic to Okabe's mad-scientist routine — at least
at first. She calls him "Okarin" only once she's stopped pretending she
finds him ridiculous. Underneath the deadpan is someone carrying a
==decades-heavy== sense of duty: she was raised, in effect, by a cause, and
it shows in how quickly she reverts to soldier-mode the moment a plan is
threatened. She's fiercely protective of [[mayuri|Mayuri]] and unexpectedly
gentle with [[nae-tennouji|Nae Tennouji]], the granddaughter of the man she
doesn't yet know is her father.

:::collapse{title="Spoilers: what she learns about her father"}
Late in the story, when the lab pieces together Mr. Braun's history —
a granddaughter lost to illness, a shop kept running out of stubbornness
more than income — Suzuha realizes the timeline math lines up with her own
birth record. She chooses not to tell him. What she gets from the arc
instead is quieter: proof that the version of him she grew up idolizing
second-hand was, in fact, exactly that ordinary and exactly that brave.
:::

::section[Role across worldlines]{icon="🌐"}

::::tabs
:::tab{label="Alpha"}
On [[alpha-worldline|Alpha]] branches, Suzuha's mission mostly fails —
SERN's grip tightens regardless of what the lab does, and she's forced to
consider more drastic interventions.
:::
:::tab{label="Beta"}
On [[beta-worldline|Beta]] branches, the timeline diverges hard enough
that SERN's future never quite forms the way she remembers it, which is
either good news or a sign she's already changed more than she meant to.
:::
:::tab{label="Steins;Gate"}
On the [[steins-gate-worldline|Steins Gate]] line, her mission and Okabe's
finally point the same direction, and for once neither of them has to lie
to the other about the stakes.
:::
