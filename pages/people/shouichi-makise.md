Shouichi Makise, publishing in disgrace under the alias **Nakabachi**, is a
failed theoretical physicist, the father of [[kurisu|Kurisu Makise]], and —
on the [[beta-worldline|Beta worldline]] — the man whose stolen conference
papers ignite World War III. He is the story's only true villain in the
domestic sense of the word: SERN is an institution, but Nakabachi is just a
man who could not forgive his own daughter for surpassing him.

::section[The fall]{icon="📉"}

His early career was genuinely distinguished — his mid-90s work on closed
timelike curves is still cited, and taught Kurisu her first physics at the
dinner table.[^dinner] The decline began when the field moved past him and
his daughter did not stop moving with it. By 2003 he had been dismissed
from his university post; by 2010 the alias, the debts, and the venue-rate
conference bookings had consumed what remained of the name Makise.

:::collapse{title="Spoilers: what happens in that room"}
On Beta-line branches, the argument in the Radio Kaikan ends with Kurisu
bleeding out on the eighth floor — the body [[okabe|Okabe]] finds in the
story's opening minutes. Nakabachi flees with her time-travel thesis to
sell abroad; the paper's contents, developed by a state program over the
following decades, produce the war that [[suzuha-amane|Suzuha's]] future is
fleeing. Every attempt Okabe makes to save Kurisu without addressing the
*paper* fails: the [[steins-gate-worldline|Steins Gate]] solution finally
threads the needle by ensuring the thesis burns while the world — and
Nakabachi himself — believes his daughter died anyway. Operation Skuld's
cruelest requirement is that ==the lie must include the liar==.
:::

::section[The authorship question]{icon="⚖️"}

:::::columns{ratio="55-45"}
::::column
The thesis Nakabachi carries out of the Radio Kaikan is Kurisu's work —
written in her notation, on her problem, extending *her* [[time-leap-machine|
time-leap]] results. His marginal annotations, recovered on one branch,
amount to renaming variables. The wiki records the paper under her
authorship and files his claim where it belongs: in the long, sour
tradition catalogued at [[wp:List of scientific priority disputes]].
::::
::::column
:::warning
Fandom shorthand calls him "Doctor Nakabachi," but no institution on any
worldline has ever verified the doctorate post-2003. The title is part of
the alias. #lore
:::

::section[Assessment]{icon="🧪"}

What makes Nakabachi durable as a villain is his scale. He has no
organization, no [[committee-of-300|Committee]], no ideology — only a
wounded vanity with a twenty-year head start. SERN needed a global
conspiracy to nearly end the world; he nearly ends it with a manila
envelope. The contrast is the point, and the reason his page sits here
among People rather than beside [[sern|SERN]] in
[[Organizations|Category: Organizations]].

[^dinner]: Kurisu's own account, related in [[episode-22]]: the last time
    physics was something they did *together* she was seven. She kept the
    notebook.
