Luka Urushibara is the gentle, perpetually apologizing child of the
Yanabayashi Shrine's keeper, [[okabe|Okabe's]] most devoted disciple in the
Hououin Kyouma fiction, and Lab Member No. 006. Their family shrine sits a
few streets from the [[future-gadget-lab|lab]], and its storeroom turns out
to hold one of the most important objects in the entire story — the IBN
5100, donated years earlier and forgotten under a tarp.[^relic]

::section[The D-Mail]{icon="📨"}

Luka's [[phone-microwave|D-Mail]] is the story's most intimate use of the
technology: a message sent back seventeen years asking their mother to eat
differently during pregnancy — folk-science, but on a worldline where it
worked. The resulting branch rewrites Luka's sex at birth, and with it a
thousand small social frictions the original Luka had carried quietly for
a lifetime.

:::warning
As with [[faris-nyannyan|Faris's]] branch, undoing this D-Mail in
[[episode-16]] required its sender's consent — a scene widely considered
the emotional center of the Alpha arc's back half. Handle spoilers gently
on this page. #lore
:::

:::collapse{title="Spoilers: the day that gets undone"}
On the branch being erased, Luka finally asks Okabe for the one thing the
worldline made possible, and gets a single day's answer before the reset.
The kindness of the writing is that the day still happened: Reading
Steiner or no, Luka chooses the original worldline knowingly, trading the
life for the shrine's relic and the lab's survival. Okabe, who remembers
everything, never once mentions it. Neither does Luka — but on every
branch afterward, Samidare's practice swings start at dawn instead of noon.
:::

::section[Swordsmanship]{icon="⚔️"}

:::::columns{ratio="60-40"}
::::column
Under Okabe's flamboyant, largely fraudulent instruction, Luka practices a
sword style Okabe invented and named "Kyouma-ryu." What neither of them
expected is that Luka got *good* — the timid posture disappears the moment
the replica blade comes out, and in the [[beta-worldline|Beta worldline's]]
darker hours that skill stops being a joke entirely.
::::
::::column
:::note
The katana "Samidare" is shrine property, technically an offering, and
absolutely not supposed to leave the grounds. It has left the grounds in
every single worldline on record.
:::

::section[The shrine]{icon="⛩️"}

Yanabayashi Shrine is small, old, and — by the standards of a district
that rebuilt itself around electronics twice in a century — almost
defiantly analog. Its storeroom ledger, maintained by hand since 1962,
is the document that eventually lets the lab trace the IBN 5100's
donation history across worldline branches, making one dusty notebook
briefly the most strategically important text in [[akihabara|Akihabara]].
The shrine also anchors the district's festival calendar; [[mayuri|
Mayuri]] has helped with the summer festival stall every year since she
was nine.

::section[Assessment]{icon="🧪"}

The wiki's editors flag Luka as the cast's best answer to a common
misreading of the show — that the D-Mails are about wish fulfillment. Every
sender learns the same lesson at different prices: ==the message changes
the world, not the sender's standing in it==. Luka's page documents the
highest price paid for that lesson, and the most grace shown in paying it.
