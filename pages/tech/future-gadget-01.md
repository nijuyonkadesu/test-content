**Future Gadget #1**, the **Bit Particle Cannon**, is the founding entry in
the [[future-gadget-lab|Future Gadget Laboratory]]'s product line and the
purest expression of the lab's design philosophy: take something mundane,
give it a name that implies war crimes, and refuse to acknowledge the gap.
Functionally, it is a high-gain universal infrared remote control shaped
like a raygun. [[okabe|Okabe]] maintains that infrared photons are
particles and that the device fires them in bits; therefore, *Bit Particle
Cannon*. The logic is airtight and everyone hates it.

::section[Design]{icon="🔧"}

::::columns{ratio="60-40"}
:::column
The Cannon's emitter stage gangs twelve IR LEDs behind a salvaged camera
lens, giving it roughly four times the effective range of a consumer
remote and a beam tight enough to switch off one specific television
across a store floor without touching its neighbors. The trigger maps to a
learning chip that can store 64 device codes; a thumb dial on the "power
regulator" (a repurposed volume knob) selects among them.

The distinctive silhouette — fins, vents, an entirely non-functional
charging coil — was vacuum-formed by [[daru|Daru]] from a model kit and
sprayed lab-signature silver. The heatsink dissipates no heat. It has
never needed to. It stays.
:::

:::note
The Cannon is the only Future Gadget ever to have *sold units* — three, at
a convention, to buyers who understood exactly what it was and wanted it
anyway. Okabe cites this as market validation. The lab's accounting
ledger, kept by [[mayuri]], records the proceeds as "snack money."
:::

::section[The physics of the bit]{icon="⚛️"}

Okabe's naming argument, reproduced here for the record: an infrared LED
emits photons of wavelength $\lambda \approx 940$ nm, each carrying energy

$$E = \frac{hc}{\lambda} \approx 1.3\ \text{eV}$$

— and since the device modulates them into *bits* (the IR protocol's
on-off keying), it is, ==in the most literal sense available to physics==,
a cannon that fires particles of bits. Peer review of this argument has
been sought from exactly one qualified physicist in the lab's orbit; her
response is preserved in [[kurisu|the reviewer]]'s own words in the lab
notebook and consists of the phrase "I'm going home."

::section[Legacy]{icon="🏆"}

As Future Gadget #1, the Cannon set the template every later gadget
follows: a real function, a fictional identity, and a build quality that
outlasts the joke. It predates the lab's descent into actual mad science
by over a year, and lab members are quietly protective of it for that
reason — it is the only gadget from ==before everything got complicated==.
It appears in the background of nearly every lab scene in the
[[episode-guide|episode guide]], hanging on its dedicated wall hook, next
to the fire extinguisher and the [[future-gadget-02|hooded fan]] that
became gadget #2. For the underlying consumer technology, see
[[wp:Remote control]].
