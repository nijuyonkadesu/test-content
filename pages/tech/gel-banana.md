# Gel Banana Phenomenon

:::infobox{title="Gel Banana" image="https://picsum.photos/seed/gelnana/300/180" caption="Exhibit A. Still attached to the bunch. Still wrong."}

| Subject | One banana (subsequently: many bananas) |

The **Gel Banana Phenomenon** is the [[future-gadget-lab|lab]]'s first
piece of experimental evidence that the [[phone-microwave|Phone
Microwave]] was doing something to causality rather than to lunch. A
banana placed in the modified microwave during a phone-linked cycle
emerged transformed: its shape preserved, its substance converted to a
uniform, faintly translucent green gel — **at room temperature**, with no
cooking odor and no measurable heating. The lab's initial classification,
"failed food experiment," survived exactly until someone noticed the
second impossibility: ==the gelled banana was back on its bunch==, across
the room, reattached at the stem.

::section[The observation]{icon="🔍"}

::::columns{ratio="60-40"}
:::column
Reconstruction from the lab notebook: a banana was placed in the drum as
improvised organic ballast during a phone-mode test. The cycle ran nine
seconds. The drum was empty afterward — noted at the time as "banana
misplaced??" — and the bunch on the table, purchased that morning, had
regrown its missing member as gel.

The sequence matters. The banana did not gel in the microwave; it
**vanished** from the microwave and **appeared, gelled, in the past
position it had occupied** before being picked from the bunch. The gel is
what a thing looks like after being sent backward in time *as matter*,
approximately one room and one hour, by a machine tuned for neither.
[[kurisu|Kurisu]]'s later analysis put it flatly: the PhoneWave's first
successful transmission wasn't a D-Mail. It was fruit.
:::
:::column
**Trial tally (bananas)**

- Unaffected: 3

:::

::section[Properties of the gel]{icon="🧪"}

| Property | Measurement | Method |
|---|---|---|
| Color | Uniform green, banana-skin hue *throughout* | Visual |
| Consistency | Firm gel; holds shape; cleaves cleanly | Chopstick, sacrificial |
| Temperature at discovery | 22.1 °C (ambient) | IR thermometer |
| Mass vs. original | −4.2% | Kitchen scale |
| Cell structure | None detectable | Daru's USB microscope |
| Odor | Faint banana, "wrong direction" (Mayuri) | Uncontrolled panel |
| Decay behavior | Does not brown; desiccates to resin | 3-week observation |

:::danger
The −4.2% mass deficit has never been accounted for. Applying $E = mc^2$
to 4.2 grams of banana yields $3.8 \times 10^{14}$ joules — which
manifestly did *not* deposit in the lab, or there would be no lab, no
[[akihabara|Akihabara]], and a substantially different wiki. Where the
mass went is the phenomenon's real open question, and the lab's file on
it is titled "DO NOT SOLVE THIS BY EXPERIMENT."
:::

::section[Significance]{icon="⚖️"}

The gel bananas are the hinge of the lab's whole story: the moment its
output changed from [[future-gadget-01|toys]] to evidence.

::::tabs
:::tab{label="Scientific"}
First reproducible temporal displacement of matter by the lab — and a
perfect miniature of [[sern-lhc|SERN's]] "jellification" wall. Matter goes
back; matter arrives *wrong*. The lab reached in nine seconds and one
banana the conclusion SERN's archives reach across three decades and
fourteen human subjects, which is either a triumph of small science or
the darkest data-efficiency argument on record.
:::
:::tab{label="Strategic"}
Recognizing the gel as SERN's jellification is what let [[kurisu|Kurisu]]
redirect the lab toward information-only payloads — the insight that made
[[d-mail|D-Mail]] work. Every worldline shift documented on this wiki
descends from someone looking at ruined fruit and taking it seriously.
:::
:::tab{label="Culinary"}
[[daru|Daru]] ate a spoonful of specimen #2 before the significance was
understood. He reports the taste as "banana, but like it's *remembering*
banana." No effects. He does not discuss it further, and the incident is
the reason the handbook has a rule zero. See
[[lab-member-handbook#rule-zero|Rule Zero]].
:::
::::

:::collapse{title="Specimen inventory"}
Three specimens persist in sealed jars on the lab's anomaly shelf, dated
and initialed. Specimen #1 has desiccated into a translucent resin the
color of old glass; #2 (bitten) is quarantined in a second, angrier jar;
#3 remains gel-state and is the control nobody controls for anything.
The [[divergence-meter|Divergence Meter]] sits on the same shelf, which
[[mayuri|Mayuri]] arranged deliberately: "the warnings should live
together." #lore
:::
