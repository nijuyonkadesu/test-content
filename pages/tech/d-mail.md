A **D-Mail** (De Lorean Mail — [[okabe|Okabe]] does not apologize for the
name) is a text message transmitted into the past via the
[[phone-microwave|Phone Microwave]]. It is the lab's founding discovery,
the trigger for every worldline shift in the lab's recorded history, and
the reason this wiki's event pages must all be annotated with divergence
values. As lab doctrine now states, in letters large enough to read from
the doorway: ==a D-Mail cannot be unsent, only outbid==.

::section[Properties]{icon="📜"}

::::columns{ratio="65-35"}
:::column
A D-Mail arrives on a real handset in the past as an ordinary message —
no glow, no fanfare, sender field garbled. The recipient acts on it (or
doesn't), causality re-knits around the new information, and the worldline
shifts to accommodate the changed history. From inside the new worldline,
the message has "always" been received; only observers with Reading
Steiner recall the version of events it overwrote.

Payload capacity is the binding constraint. The PhoneWave's aperture
stays open for microseconds, enough for roughly **36 bytes** per burst.
Longer messages fragment into multiple bursts that can arrive out of
order, minutes apart — the lab's second D-Mail arrived as three fragments
across two hours, and the recipient read them in exactly the wrong
order.[^1]
:::
:::column
**Anatomy of a send**

1. Compose SMS on docked handset
2. Set target time offset
3. Microwave runs (do not open)
4. Message ceases to exist *here*
5. World quietly rewrites itself
:::
::::

::section[Rules of thumb]{icon="📏"}

The lab's accumulated, expensively-learned guidance:

| Rule | Reason | Learned in |
|---|---|---|
| Never send about money | Divergence explodes; SERN flags gambling anomalies | [[episode-05\|Episode 05]] |
| Never send about people's lives | The attractor field routes around it, cruelly | [[episode-12\|Episode 12]] |
| Shorter is safer | Fragmentation scrambles meaning | [[episode-03\|Episode 03]] |
| Photograph the [[divergence-meter\|meter]] first | Only way to measure what you did | [[episode-06\|Episode 06]] |
| The undo mail must contradict, not delete | The past cannot un-know; it can only be re-persuaded | [[episode-13\|Episode 13]] |

:::tip
An "undo mail" does not restore the original worldline — it moves to a
*third* worldline that closely resembles the first. The
[[divergence-meter|Divergence Meter]] readings before and after an undo
never match exactly. The difference has never been observed to be zero.
:::

::section[Notable D-Mails]{icon="📮"}

::::tabs
:::tab{label="The first"}
A one-line test message. It arrived one week in the past, and the shift it
caused was large enough that the lab's founding membership *changed* —
the canonical example of how disproportionate the effect can be. Covered
in [[episode-01|Episode 01]] and [[episode-02|Episode 02]].
:::
:::tab{label="The worst"}
A lottery-number mail sent on behalf of a friend. The numbers arrived
garbled, the winnings never materialized, and the divergence cost took
three undo mails to claw back. See [[episode-10|Episode 10]].
:::
:::tab{label="The last"}
The final D-Mail sent from the lab was an undo mail — the one that pulled
the worldline out of the [[alpha-worldline|Alpha attractor field]]
entirely. Its 12 characters are redacted on this wiki by unanimous lab
vote. [[episode-13|Episode 13]].
:::
::::

:::collapse{title="Why 'De Lorean'?"}
Okabe named it after the time-travelling car from a film he claims to have
watched "for research." Attempts to rename it ("Retro-SMS," "chronomail,"
Daru's unprintable suggestion) all failed to stick. The abbreviations table
on this wiki lists D-Mail as canonical, so the argument is officially
over.
:::

::section[Detection and interception]{icon="🛰️"}

Every D-Mail transits the public cellular network for the mundane half of
its journey, which is how SERN's monitoring net caught the lab so quickly
— the temporal burst is exotic, but the SMS itself is just an SMS. Later
sends were routed through a chain of anonymizing relays Daru assembled,
which bought weeks at most. The uncomfortable lesson, recorded in the
[[lab-member-handbook|handbook]]: *information that changes the past is
still information, and information leaks.* For the theoretical framing of
why worldlines converge despite interference, see
[[attractor-field|Attractor field theory]] and the overview at
[[wp:Many-worlds interpretation]].
