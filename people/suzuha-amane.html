Suzuha Amane is a time traveler from the year 2036, sent to the present by a resistance cell fighting a global order enforced by SERN. She spends most of 2010 undercover as a part-time clerk at Braun Tsuushin, the CRT-repair shop run by an old man she knows only as her employer — and who is, unbeknownst to either of them for most of the story, her own father, Yuugo Tennouji.

She later joins the Future Gadget Laboratory as Lab Member No. 004, bringing a soldier's directness that clashes, then gels, with Okabe's theatrics. Her mission is simple to state and brutal to execute: stop the timeline that produces the world she was born into — a world under martial rule, its populace surveilled through technology descended from Future Gadget Lab research that SERN captured and weaponized.

🎯Mission

Suzuha's future is one where SERN, having perfected time travel decades ahead of anyone else, used that head start to consolidate total control. Her cell's plan hinges on preventing SERN from ever recovering the Phone Microwave's underlying research — which means staying close to the one lab reckless enough to keep reinventing it. She carries a time machine capable of one-way jumps into the past; getting home again is a separate, much harder problem she does not discuss with the lab until she has to.

Her public cover story borrows the name John Titor — a nod to an old internet legend about a soldier from 2036 who claimed to have traveled back looking for an IBN 5100-class computer.1 Lab members who catch the reference never quite believe the coincidence is a coincidence.

Suzuha never volunteers her real objective outright. Most of what the lab learns about 2036 comes out sideways, in offhand remarks she immediately tries to walk back.

Her bicycle isn't decoration — in at least one worldline it's how she wins a race against a car through central Akihabara, and how she buys Okabe the seconds he needs at the climax of the July incident.

🎭Personality

Blunt, athletic, and allergic to Okabe's mad-scientist routine — at least at first. She calls him "Okarin" only once she's stopped pretending she finds him ridiculous. Underneath the deadpan is someone carrying a decades-heavy sense of duty: she was raised, in effect, by a cause, and it shows in how quickly she reverts to soldier-mode the moment a plan is threatened. She's fiercely protective of Mayuri and unexpectedly gentle with Nae Tennouji, the granddaughter of the man she doesn't yet know is her father.

Late in the story, when the lab pieces together Mr. Braun's history — a granddaughter lost to illness, a shop kept running out of stubbornness more than income — Suzuha realizes the timeline math lines up with her own birth record. She chooses not to tell him. What she gets from the arc instead is quieter: proof that the version of him she grew up idolizing second-hand was, in fact, exactly that ordinary and exactly that brave.

🌐Role across worldlines

On Alpha branches, Suzuha's mission mostly fails — SERN's grip tightens regardless of what the lab does, and she's forced to consider more drastic interventions.

On Beta branches, the timeline diverges hard enough that SERN's future never quite forms the way she remembers it, which is either good news or a sign she's already changed more than she meant to.

On the Steins Gate line, her mission and Okabe's finally point the same direction, and for once neither of them has to lie to the other about the stakes.
